# Unit 01 — Hello, Friends!

## Product Direction

**World:** Sunbeam School on the first week of class. The recurring child characters are Maya, Leo, Jun, and Nia; **Pip**, a tiny teal bird carrying a gold bell, is the friendly guide. The world uses one connected school setting—front door, classroom, photo corner, friendship garden, and party room—so that every interaction is meaningful in the same social world. A learner’s language moves the story forward: friends enter, labels find their owners, detective clues reveal names, a garden grows, and the class party fills with new friends.

**Art system:** Wide 1920 × 1080 storybook-vector boards. Primary background register is a warm cream classroom with deep-navy architectural framing. Sky blue, mint, coral, golden yellow, and lavender identify distinct task zones. Character proportions, hand-drawn sunburst accents, rounded type, paper-cut shadows, directional arrows, and a small lower-left progress compass recur across the unit. Boards use scene-integrated task surfaces—doors, lockers, ribbon routes, a photo booth, garden beds, party tables—not generic worksheets.

**Static-board limitation:** The images are finished visual boards. Where a board implies a drag, reveal, or text-entry activity, the accompanying lesson handoff specifies the native action required in the teaching platform; the static render is not presented as a functional board.

| Lesson | World mission | New focus | Input → practice → output trajectory | Visible final proof |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open Sunbeam School and welcome each friend. | hello, hi, goodbye, bye, friend | Notice arrival/departure; classify greeting and farewell; deliver a greeting and a farewell. | Learner’s wave token completes the welcome parade. |
| 2 | Return lost name tags to the correct friends. | name, I, am, my; **My name is / I’m** | Notice name labels; build both identity frames; create and say a name tag. | A personal name tag appears on the class display. |
| 3 | Become a name detective and collect friend clues. | what, your, name, ask; **What’s your name?** | Hear the question; pair question with answer; conduct a partner information exchange. | A detective card carries a partner’s confirmed name. |

| 5 | Welcome every guest to the class party. | integrated review | Refresh each language function; sequence a full exchange; independently perform and document a conversation. | A completed class party mural with greetings, names, meeting language, and farewells. |

## Language Contract

| Language target | Visual support | Valid learner proof | Avoided risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hello! / Hi! | Arrival door, wave, morning sunlight | “Hello!” or “Hi!” to a peer token | Treating *hello* and *hi* as different meanings. |
| Goodbye! / Bye! | Leaving door, backpack, setting sun | “Goodbye!” or “Bye!” as a friend departs | Using a farewell at the arrival gate. |
| My name is ___. | Large coloured sentence ribbon with one name slot | “My name is [name].” | Leaving the learner with only a written label and no oral model. |
| I’m ___. | `I am` magnetic tiles transform to `I’m` | “I’m [name].” | Presenting `I’m` without the `I am` connection. |
| What’s your name? | Gold question ribbon points to a friend | A question with rising intonation, then wait | Requiring a name answer before the question has been modeled. |
| Nice to meet you. / Nice to meet you, too. | Two flower petals / two linked hands | First line plus exact reciprocal reply | Showing `too` as an isolated vocabulary item without a reply role. |

## Board Map and Interaction Contract

### Lesson 1 — Hello and Goodbye

| # | Board title and learner job | Classroom interaction/state rule | Required spoken proof |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sunbeam School Opens:** point to the door and predict the welcome mission. | Teacher reveals the bell; no answer visible initially. | “Hello!” |
| 2 | **Who Is Coming?** find arriving friends, then leaving friends in a split-time school scene. | Tap/point to arrival or departure trails; reveal correct language only after choice. | “Hello!” / “Goodbye!” |
| 3 | **Wave Words Pop!** connect five illustrated moments to hello, hi, goodbye, bye, friend. | Use five large word bubbles as reveal cards, not a pre-labelled match. | Teacher-led echo of all five words. |
| 4 | **Hello Door / Goodbye Door:** send each character along the correct door path. | Character tokens snap to one of two door mats and remain there. | “Hi, Leo!” or “Bye, Maya!” |
| 5 | **Pip’s Echo Hall:** choose the matching wave, small wave, big goodbye wave, or friend high-five. | Point/gesture then repeat; language chip lights up on confirm. | “Hello!” / “Hi!” / “Goodbye!” / “Bye!” |
| 6 | **The Bell Route:** build a route from arrival bell to leaving bell. | Move greeting/farewell tokens onto a floor path; final route forms a complete school day. | “Hello, friend!” then “Goodbye, friend!” |
| 7 | **Story: A New Friend:** follow Maya and Leo from doorway to home-time. | Click three numbered story beats; caption strips remain on the same side. | “Hi!” / “Bye!” |
| 8 | **Wave Circle:** choose a character, greet the group, and return a farewell. | A learner token moves to the centre and receives a wave sticker after both utterances. | “Hello!” … “Goodbye!” |
| 9 | **Welcome Mat Challenge:** roleplay a short arrival and leaving at the classroom mat. | Pair A enters, Pair B waves; switch roles once. | “Hi!” / “Bye!” |
| 10 | **Wave Parade Exit:** add one coloured wave to Pip’s banner. | Teacher checks a greeting and farewell before awarding a wave. | “Hello!” plus “Goodbye!” |

### Lesson 2 — My Name Is…

| # | Board title and learner job | Classroom interaction/state rule | Required spoken proof |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Lost Name Tags:** help Pip find four name tags. | Four blank lanyards hang beside character silhouettes. | “Name.” |
| 2 | **Whose Tag Is It?** predict the owner from picture clues. | Reveal the printed name after an owner choice. | “[Maya’s] name.” |

| 4 | **I Am → I’m:** watch sentence magnets click together. | `I` + `am` magnetically fold into `I’m`; selected name completes the phrase. | “I’m Leo.” |
| 5 | **Mirror Model:** copy Maya’s clear self-introduction in a photo mirror. | Turn a mirror frame toward one learner; teacher reveals model after an attempt. | “My name is __.” |
| 6 | **Tag Workshop:** attach each named tag to its friend. | Tags snap to matching lanyards and remain visible. | “My name is Nia.” |
| 7 | **Make My Tag:** create a personal tag with a chosen colour, star, and editable name. | Learner choices persist on a tag; teacher enters or learner writes their name. | “My name is [name].” |
| 8 | **Photo Booth Take 1:** pick `My name is` or `I’m` and speak into the microphone. | A flash effect follows spoken production; no single “correct” frame required. | “I’m [name].” |
| 9 | **Welcome Line:** greet a classmate and introduce yourself at the classroom door. | Pair role cards structure turns; then roles switch. | “Hi! My name is [name].” |
| 10 | **Name Gallery Exit:** place the finished tag on the Sunbeam wall. | Teacher checks one complete sentence before placement. | “My name is [name].” |

## Quality Gates Applied to Every Lesson

Each lesson introduces all stated vocabulary in an unambiguous one-to-one visual before production; maintains one primary visual register; uses large high-contrast copy; shows a single dominant learner job per board; provides a visible action-to-payoff state; and closes with an observable spoken production rather than an empty celebration. The final production task in each lesson carries its exact language proof and teacher/peer check.
